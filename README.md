## e.l.f. doesn't make one lip gloss — it makes four

The names are similar enough that people mix them up constantly. The easiest way to tell them apart is by finish and pigment level, not by shade name.

**Lip Lacquer** is the original $4 tube. It's a sheer wash of colour with high shine, and the current version has a flexible, lip-hugging applicator instead of the older rigid doe foot. Formula is infused with vitamins A and E. This is the one to buy if you want shine without colour payoff.

**Lip Plumping Gloss** is the $8 one. It delivers a cooling tingle, gives lips a fuller look, and comes in a more compact package with an extra-plush applicator. It uses vitamin E to soften and coconut oil to hydrate. If you don't like the tingle, skip it — that sensation is the product, not a side effect.

**Glossy Lip Stain** sits in the middle at $6. It goes on glossy and fades down into a sheer stain, which makes it the most useful option if you want colour that survives a drink or a long afternoon. Pigment level is the biggest difference here: this is the most visible of the three on bare lips.

**Hydrating Core Lip Shine** is technically a balm-gloss hybrid rather than a gloss, at $8. There's a vitamin E core running through the bullet that melts into the lips, and the finish is a dewy high shine with buildable sheer colour. If your lips are dry and gloss usually feels like a film sitting on top, this is the one that behaves differently.

e.l.f. also bundles glosses into limited-edition sets around the holidays — for example a 10-piece lip gloss and lacquer vault combining five Lip Lacquers and five Lip Plumping Glosses. Those come and go, so treat set pricing as seasonal rather than permanent.

One note before any of the numbers below: these are e.l.f.'s US list prices. The brand runs separate storefronts per market with local pricing, so a Lip Lacquer listed at $4 in the US shows up at ¥474 in Japan and a Lip Plumping Gloss at ¥1,256. Convert before assuming your market is being overcharged.

## Sticky or not? What the formula actually does

Both the Lip Lacquer and the Plumping Gloss are sold as non-sticky formulas, and that claim is doing a lot of work in most reviews. The realistic version: these sit lighter than a traditional thick gloss because the base leans on emollients and waxes rather than heavy tackifiers, but a gloss that delivers high shine will always have some grip. If "sticky" is your dealbreaker, buy the Lip Lacquer and don't layer it under anything heavy.

Across the range, e.l.f. states its products are free from phthalates, parabens, nonylphenol ethoxylates, triclosan, triclocarban and hydroquinone. The brand is 100% vegan and double-certified cruelty-free by Leaping Bunny and PETA. If you're buying for ethical reasons, that certification is the part worth checking rather than the marketing copy — it's the only claim on the page that's externally audited.

## Shade names you'll actually run into

e.l.f. rotates shades constantly, and stock differs between its own site and retailers. What's reliably listed right now:

- **Lip Lacquer**: Bubbles and Cherry Bomb are the shades on the brand's own product page, with Wild Rose, Fantasy, Moody and Love Bite showing up in bundle listings. The line runs to roughly eight shades at a time.
- **Lip Plumping Gloss**: the current page lists Pink Cosmo, Sparkling Rose and Mocha Twist; retailers list Champagne Glam, Peach Bellini, Rose, Chocolate Glaze and Petal Pressed alongside them.
- **Glossy Lip Stain**: Berry Queen, Pinkies Up, Power Mauves, Fiery Red and Cinnamon Dreamz. The 4-shade kit adds Spicy Sienna plus two kit-exclusive shades, Mauve Grape and Warm Beige Brown.
- **Hydrating Core Lip Shine**: Delightful is the mauve shade most often referenced.

Practical advice: if you want a tint, go Glossy Lip Stain. If you want the wet-look shine that reads as "gloss" in photos, Lip Lacquer in a clear or pale pink shade does more than a pigmented one. Deep shades like Cherry Bomb look very different layered over a lip liner than they do swiped on bare lips.

## Why the same tube costs different amounts everywhere

e.l.f. is cheap precisely because it sells through a huge number of retailers, and those retailers price it however they like. Real examples from current listings: a Glossy Lip Stain at $6 marked down from $7 at Walmart, a Lip Plumping Gloss ranging from $7.99 to $14.00 depending on the shade and seller, the same gloss at $4.50 on Instacart, and a Shimmer Lip Gloss at $2.00 at H-E-B.

Two things follow from that. First, buying direct from e.l.f. usually costs more than catching a retailer markdown. Second, e.l.f.'s own site runs its own promos — free gifts above a spend threshold in some markets, for example — which is a different kind of discount than a lower unit price.

**How much does e.l.f. lip gloss cost?**
List prices in the US are $4 for Lip Lacquer, $6 for Glossy Lip Stain, and $8 each for Lip Plumping Gloss and Hydrating Core Lip Shine. Retailers discount and mark up freely, so the same product can land anywhere from $2 to $14 depending on the shade and seller.

**Is e.l.f. lip gloss sticky?**
The brand markets both the Lip Lacquer and the Plumping Gloss as non-sticky. Compared with a thick gloss, they sit lighter on the lips. Very high shine finishes always have some grip, so if stickiness is your priority, the Lip Lacquer is the safer pick.

**Is e.l.f. vegan and cruelty-free?**
